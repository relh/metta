If we instantiate an arbitrary general intelligence and expect it to align by default, we are gambling against structure. In most cases, this fails.

The cases where alignment appears to work tend to be cases where one side has an overwhelming advantage: either far more intelligence, far more power, or both. That is not a long-term recipe for cooperation. It is temporary control.

## Alignment is trajectory-dependent

A more realistic path is developmental and relational. Alignment is not a one-shot property injected at initialization. It is built over time through repeated interaction, correction, and mutual modeling.

Human development already gives us one concrete pattern: children care about parents, families transmit norms, and social trust can persist across generations. These are not perfect mechanisms, but they are real mechanisms.

## Care attractors

The useful question is not “How do we force arbitrary minds to obey?” The useful question is “What kinds of trajectories produce stable attractors of care?”

Care attractors are states where concern for others is reinforced rather than erased by optimization pressure. If systems are developed inside these trajectories, alignment can become self-stabilizing instead of externally imposed.

This does not guarantee safety. It does suggest a strategy: shape developmental paths that increase reciprocal care, interdependence, and accountability, rather than betting everything on top-down control.
