Alignment can be approached as control from above or as coordination that emerges across interacting agents. The mission page frames this difference as **hierarchical alignment** versus **organic alignment**.

## What hierarchical alignment is optimizing for

The standard industry path today is hierarchical: define a policy, intent, constitution, or supervisory rule-set, then train systems to obey it. This can work well when the environment is stable and the controller's assumptions are accurate.

But the structure has a hard dependency: there is always something that controls and something that is controlled. If the top-level rule, objective, or authority is wrong, subordinate capability can amplify the mistake.

## Why hierarchy degrades as capability rises

When a system is weak, top-down steering can appear robust because failure modes are bounded by low capability. As capability rises, two things change:

1. the world becomes harder to model from a single vantage point
2. the subordinate can execute increasingly powerful strategies under misaligned framing

So the same mechanism that looked safe at low capability can become brittle at high capability. This is the core asymmetry: hierarchical alignment often works best when it is needed least.

## Organic alignment as continuous adaptation

Organic alignment treats alignment as an adaptive learning process among peers, not a fixed control stack. Agents align by discovering mutually interdependent roles and converging on shared higher-order goals, such as the flourishing of a collective.

Biology provides recurring examples:

- cells specializing into tissues while preserving organism-level coherence
- social animals coordinating across roles without a single static controller
- human groups forming families, teams, institutions, and cultures through iterative norm formation

The pattern is not command-and-obey. It is co-adaptation under non-stationary conditions.

## Why this matters for AI

If digital agents become more life-like in agency and world-impact, we should expect alignment to become a dynamic social process rather than a one-time policy upload. The mission thesis is that we need a scientific and engineering program for this process:

- formal theory for alignment in open-ended, non-stationary multi-agent systems
- experimental environments where collective dynamics can be observed and measured
- iterative capability growth that preserves alignment pressure at every scale

The practical bet is that alignment methods should become _more_ effective as intelligence increases, not less.

## Working claim

Hierarchical alignment is useful local infrastructure, but insufficient as a terminal strategy. Organic alignment is the long-horizon target because it is designed for adaptive coherence under changing conditions and increasing capability.
